What it does

Built for the way repertorisation actually works: find the narrow rubric, keep it, discard the rest, and let the materia medica decide.

Search in repertory language

Type anxiety night or thirstless and get the exact rubrics. Results are ranked narrowest first — the rubrics with three remedies are the ones that narrow a case.

Every grade preserved

Each remedy keeps its printed grade — 3 strongest, 2 moderate, 1 least — so one narrow grade-3 rubric can outrank a remedy scattered across five broad ones.

Case & repertorisation

Add the rubrics that survive. The analysis ranks remedies by the sum of grades and how many rubrics they cover, and you can exclude a remedy to see who is next.

Remedy × rubric matrix

See the whole case at a glance: which remedy scores what, in which rubric, at which grade.

Export and print

Plain text, CSV, JSON, or a clean print/PDF sheet — with your own notes on each rubric. Everything is generated locally.

Genuinely offline

One self-contained HTML file. Save it, open it on a plane, install it as an app. Nothing you type leaves the device.

How to repertorise with it

Five steps, and the fifth one matters most.

  1. Search in repertory languageA patient says “anxious at night and can’t sleep”. The repertory says anxiety night.
  2. Prefer narrow rubricsA rubric with 250 remedies tells you almost nothing. One with three is gold. Turn on Narrow only to see them.
  3. Add what survivesCollect the rubrics that genuinely describe this patient — not every one that half-fits.
  4. Read the analysisRemedies ranked by the sum of grades, and by how many of your rubrics each one covers.
  5. Then open the materia medicaThe ranking is a shortlist to study, not an answer. The repertory narrows; the materia medica decides.

What this text is — precisely

Repertorium Publicum (2008) by Vladimir Polony — an open, Kent-derived repertory published as part of the OpenRep source code.

It is not Kent’s Repertory of the Homoeopathic Materia Medica, 1897 first edition. It has its own chapter structure — separate Clinical, Appetite, Blood and Heart & Circulation chapters, which Kent does not have — and its own additions.

The dataset is licensed GPL v3, not public domain, and the app is distributed under GPL v3 because it embeds that data. Kent’s original 1897 text is public domain; this compilation is not.

Questions

Do I need an internet connection?

No. The whole repertory is embedded in a single HTML file and decompressed in your browser. Open it once, and it works on a plane, in a clinic with no signal, or from a USB stick.

Is this Kent’s Repertory?

It is Kent-derived — the rubric structure, the language and the grading all follow Kent — but it is Repertorium Publicum (2008), an open repertory with its own chapter structure and additions. If you need the 1897 first edition specifically, cite the edition and use a source that states it.

Does my case data go anywhere?

No. There is no server, no account and no network request. Your case is kept in your browser’s local storage on that device, and exports are generated locally as files you control.

Can I use it commercially?

The app and its embedded data are under GPL v3, which permits commercial use. It requires that derivative works also be licensed under GPL v3 and that the source is made available. See the repository.

Is this medical advice?

No. It is a study and reference tool for practitioners and students, and not a substitute for the judgement of a qualified practitioner.
